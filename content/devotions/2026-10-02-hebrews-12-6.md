# Morning Mercy

## Hebrews 12:6

*“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.”* — Hebrews 12:6 (KJV)

I remember living in my fallen state, near death or close to it every day. The Holy Spirit seemed to be convicting me all the time. I couldn’t feel any encouragement. Whatever I did right, or thought I did right, all I could see were my mistakes and how bad things could get. And some things did get very bad.

I had heard half the sentence: “For the wages of sin is death.” I knew what my choices were costing me. It took me a long time to hear the rest: “but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord” (Romans 6:23).

The warning was never the whole message. God was offering me life through His Son.

Hebrews contrasts our earthly fathers’ imperfect discipline with God’s purpose: “but he for our profit, that we might be partakers of his holiness” (Hebrews 12:10). Human fathers can get correction wrong. God’s correction is never careless or cruel.

Even the hard word “scourgeth” reminds us that His discipline can be painful. But the passage points toward holiness and “the peaceable fruit of righteousness” (Hebrews 12:11). It gives no earthly father permission to be cruel.

God’s discipline does not mean He has stopped loving His children. Hebrews asks, “for what son is he whom the father chasteneth not?” (Hebrews 12:7). His correction calls us to endure, listen, and grow.

And love must remain at the center:

*“And now abideth faith, hope, charity, these three; but the greatest of these is charity.”* — 1 Corinthians 13:13 (KJV)

Hear the warning. Receive the gift. Let love remain.

### Prayer

Father, thank You for loving me and calling me away from sin. When Your correction stings, help me trust Your purpose and remember that I am more than my mistakes. Thank You for the gift of eternal life through Jesus Christ. Teach me to receive correction with humility and to correct others with patience and love. In Jesus’ name, amen.

# Morning Mercies Book Devotional

## Hebrews 12:6 Meaning: Whom the Lord Loveth He Chasteneth

### A Morning Mercies story on Hebrews 12:6 (KJV)

*The following fictional narrative illustrates the truth of the passage.*

The bus from Montgomery was running forty minutes late. James sat under the terminal lights, waiting for his son to come home.

A boy dropped onto the bench across from him. He couldn’t have been more than fifteen or sixteen. He held a worn duffel bag tight against his shoulder. James knew that look right away. He had worn it himself.

“Going somewhere?” James asked.

“Yeah. I’m out of here.”

“Why in such a hurry?”

“I’m not living under the thumb of a tyrant anymore.”

“Something wrong at home?” James asked softly.

“Not anymore. According to everybody at home, the thing that was wrong just left.”

The boy’s jaw tightened.

“I’m tired of being the problem in my house. My dad doesn’t even act like he loves me. All he does is tell me what I’m doing wrong. I work hard for my grades. I try to be the best person I can. It’s never enough for him. It’s never going to be enough.”

James sat quietly for a while. The lights hummed.

“When you say you don’t feel loved,” he said finally, “I believe that’s how it feels. I won’t try to talk you out of that. But tell me something. Would you be safe if you went home?”

The boy looked at the floor.

“I’m safe. He doesn’t hit me.”

He paused.

“He’s always been there, too. He comes to my games. We’ve never gone without food or anything I needed. Half the stuff I wanted, he found a way to get.”

He pulled at the strap of his bag.

“But when I bring home a good grade, he finds the one I missed.”

James nodded.

“You can be thankful he stayed and still be honest about how his words hurt you. Both things are true.”

He leaned forward.

“Here’s what I’ve learned, though. When hurt goes long enough without being heard, you can start believing it tells the whole story: I’m not loved. That’s a hard thing to live under, son. I lived under it for years.”

He took a small Bible out of his coat pocket.

“There was a time I was nearly dying every day, and I ran from every voice that corrected me. Some of those voices really were cruel. But sometimes I was headed somewhere dangerous and wouldn’t listen. I kept hearing one warning without its ending.”

He read it to the boy:

*“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”*

James kept his finger on the page.

“That’s Romans 6:23. I knew all about what my choices cost. It took me a long time to hear that God was offering me life.”

He turned a few pages.

“Listen to this.”

*“My son, despise not thou the chastening of the Lord, nor faint when thou art rebuked of him: For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth.”*

“That’s from Hebrews 12,” James said. “God corrects His children because they’re His. A little farther down, it says His purpose is that we share in His holiness. His correction has a good purpose, even when it hurts.”

The boy looked up.

“So you’re saying my dad’s right?”

“I’m saying God is faithful. Your dad may be trying to do right by you and still be getting things wrong. God’s goodness doesn’t make every word your father says good.”

James closed the Bible halfway.

“If there’s truth in a correction, you can listen to that truth. And if the way he speaks is hurting you, that needs to be heard, too.”

The boy looked toward the bus doors.

“He’ll just say I’m arguing.”

“He might,” James said. “Is there somebody you trust who could sit down with you both? An aunt, a coach, your pastor? You don’t have to fix everything tonight. Just don’t disappear without letting someone safe know where you are.”

The boy took out his phone and looked at it for a long moment. Then he typed:

*Can you come get me? I need help talking to Dad.*

James waited until the message went through. Then he opened the Bible to one more page.

“Paul wrote this.”

*“And now abideth faith, hope, charity, these three; but the greatest of these is charity.”*

“Charity means love,” James said. “Your father’s correction needs love in how he says it. Your hurt needs someone to hear it. Whatever happens when you two talk, don’t either of you leave love out.”

The boy put the duffel down at his feet. He was still angry, and he was afraid of the conversation ahead. But his aunt was on her way.

Air brakes hissed outside.

The Montgomery bus pulled in, and a young man in his twenties stepped off and looked around the terminal. James stood up. His eyes were wet.

“That’s mine,” he said. “Took him a while to come home. Took me a while, too.”

The boy watched the two men hold each other in the doorway for a long time.

Hear the warning. Receive the gift. Let love remain.

*“Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.”* — Hebrews 12:11 (KJV)

*May the Father who loves you give you strength to receive His correction and grow in His peace.*

### Prayer

Father, thank You for loving us and calling us away from sin. Forgive us when we despise Your correction, and help us recognize Your faithful care. Heal the wounds left by harsh words and failed love. Give us humility to listen, courage to speak truth, and patience with one another. Thank You for eternal life through Jesus Christ. Let love be the strongest thing in our homes. In Jesus’ name, amen.
