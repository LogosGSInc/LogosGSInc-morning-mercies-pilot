# Morning Mercy

## Philippians 4:19 (KJV)

*"But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus."*

Paul wrote these words from prison to a church that had just sent him a gift through Epaphroditus. Verses earlier, he said he had learned contentment in plenty and in hunger, in abundance and in want (Philippians 4:11–12). Then he turns and blesses this generous church in return: the God they trusted with their giving will care for them too.

"According to his riches" points to the source and sufficiency of God's supply. It is not a formula for a certain amount, timing, or form. The Greek word Paul uses for *supply*, *plēroō*, carries the sense of filling or fulfilling — but how God fulfills each need is His to decide. Paul himself still hungered at times.

The deeper assurance sits earlier in Scripture: *"He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?"* (Romans 8:32). The Father who gave the greatest gift is not a stingy Father. His love toward His people in Christ is settled — even when the answer to a particular need does not arrive when or how we hoped.

And that same grace equips us to be generous: *"And God is able to make all grace abound toward you; that ye, always having all sufficiency in all things, may abound to every good work"* (2 Corinthians 9:8). Provision is meant to move through us, not stop with us.

**Look to Christ. Trust His care. Live open-handed.**

### Prayer

Father, thank You for giving Your Son — the settled proof of Your love. Teach me to trust You rather than measure You by my shortages. Meet me as You see fit, and make me generous with whatever You place in my hands, that Your grace may reach others through me. In Jesus' name, amen.

# Morning Mercies Book Devotional

## Philippians 4:19 Meaning: My God Shall Supply All Your Need

### A Morning Mercies story on Philippians 4:19 (KJV)

*The following is a fictional narrative written to illustrate the truth of the passage.*

Lena counted the bills twice on the pharmacy counter. Then a third time, hoping the number might change. It didn't. She was forty-two dollars short.

Behind her, a woman set down a basket and waited. Lena turned to apologize, embarrassed. "I'm sorry. I'll just—" She started to hand the pharmacist back the small white bag. Six months without her husband, and every month still surprised her.

"Ring hers with mine," the woman said quietly.

"Oh, no. Please. I couldn't."

"You can," the woman said. "I've been where you're standing. Come have coffee with me next door when you're done."

Her name was June. They sat in a corner booth while rain moved across the parking lot. June pulled a small worn Bible from her purse and set it on the table between them.

"I don't want you to leave here thinking God always pays every bill through a stranger," June said. "But let me show you something Paul wrote."

She turned to Philippians. "Chapter four, verse nineteen. Read it aloud."

Lena's voice was thin. *"But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus."*

"Paul said that to a church that had sent him a gift while he was in prison," June said. "A few verses earlier, he said he knew both fullness and hunger. Their kindness mattered. So did his trust in God when he still lacked."

Lena rested her hands around the warm cup. "Then what does 'all your need' mean?"

"God's care comes from His riches in Christ. Paul doesn't tell us its timing or shape." June paused. "I needed help once and nobody came to the counter. Some days I had to ask the church. I hated asking. They brought groceries and sat with me. Other troubles lasted longer."

Lena looked out at the rain. She had been afraid that accepting the medicine meant owing June an answer, or a promise that tomorrow would be easier. June asked for neither.

"There's a verse in Romans I return to," June said, turning a few pages. "Chapter eight, verse thirty-two."

*"He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?"*

"That's where I rest," June said. "God gave His Son. Hardship doesn't undo His love."

They sat a while longer. Before they left, June showed her another passage: *"And God is able to make all grace abound toward you; that ye, always having all sufficiency in all things, may abound to every good work"* (2 Corinthians 9:8). The offering Paul was gathering would help believers in need. Grace could move from one open hand to another.

Nine months later, Lena stopped at that same pharmacy. A young woman ahead of her was counting singles, cheeks red. Lena remembered June's voice, but she did not give a speech. She asked the pharmacist how much remained, paid what she could, and left her number in case the woman wanted to talk. On her way out, Lena thanked God for the help she had received, and asked Him to care for them both in the days ahead.

**Look to Christ. Trust His care. Live open-handed.**

*May the Father who gave His Son teach you to trust Him in plenty and in want.*

### Prayer

Father, we thank You for the gift of Your Son — proof beyond argument that You love Your people. Forgive our habit of measuring You by our shortages. Give us what we need, in the form and the timing You choose, and meet those who go without today with strength, help, and hope. Teach us contentment in plenty and in want, and make our hands open toward others as You have been open toward us. In Jesus' name we pray, amen.
