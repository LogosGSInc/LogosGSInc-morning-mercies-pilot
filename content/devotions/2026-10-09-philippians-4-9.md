# Morning Mercy

DO THE THINGS YOU'VE SEEN
Morning Mercies | Authored by Grace

"Those things, which ye have both learned, and received, and heard, and seen in me, do: and the God of peace shall be with you."
— Philippians 4:9 (KJV)

Most of us learned the important things by watching. How to bait a hook. How to hold a baby. How to say "I was wrong" and mean it.

Paul wrote this letter as a prisoner, to a church he had planted. Acts tells how that church began. Paul and Silas were beaten and put in a Philippian jail with their feet in the stocks: "And at midnight Paul and Silas prayed, and sang praises unto God: and the prisoners heard them" (Acts 16:25). Now Paul writes to the believers there: what you learned from me, what was handed to you, what you heard, what you saw me do. Do it.

That isn't bragging. One chapter earlier he admits, "Not as though I had already attained, either were already perfect" (from Philippians 3:12). Elsewhere he says, "Be ye followers of me, even as I also am of Christ" (1 Corinthians 11:1). Paul's life points past himself to Jesus.

And notice who he's writing to. People God had already begun a good work in (Philippians 1:6). Doing these things doesn't buy God's presence. Jesus already paid for us. But Paul does join a promise to a practice: do these things, "and the God of peace shall be with you." Just before, he named some of them: pray about everything with thanksgiving (4:6), and think on whatever is true, honest, just, pure, and lovely (4:8).

Paul wasn't writing about addiction, and he gives no promise that trouble ends. He was still a prisoner when he wrote about peace. But by application, recovery is learned a lot like faith: from God's Word, and from faithful people you can watch up close.

TODAY'S STEP
Think of one thing you've seen a faithful, trustworthy person do. Pray before a hard call. Show up early. Tell the truth when a lie would be easier. Do that one thing today.

PRAYER
Father, thank You for the work You began in me through Jesus. I know more than I do. Help me practice what I have learned and seen. I bring You today's worries with thanks. God of peace, be with me. In Jesus' name, amen.

# Morning Mercies Book Devotional

Odell's Small Engine sat between a feed store and a church that used to be a bank, and on Saturday mornings it smelled like coffee, gas, and cut grass.

Benny had been coming Saturdays for three months. Five months sober. Odell paid him twenty dollars and a sausage biscuit to sweep up, and taught him engines for free.

This morning a riding mower sat on the lift, its carburetor in pieces on a shop towel, and Benny had been staring at the same little brass part for ten minutes.

"You gonna clean that jet or marry it?" Odell called from the counter.

"Sorry." Benny picked up the spray can. Set it back down. "My girl turns seven tomorrow."

Odell put his coffee down. "Party?"

"At her grandma's. Her mom said I can come for an hour." Benny wiped his hands on a rag dirtier than his hands. "The whole family'll be there. They all remember the last birthday I showed up to. I didn't sleep."

Odell came around the counter, slow on his bad knee, and leaned on the workbench. He nodded at his red toolbox. Taped inside the lid was an index card, soft as cloth, the ink gone brown. Benny had seen it a hundred times and never read it.

"Read me that."

Square, careful letters.

> "Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus."
> — Philippians 4:6-7 · KJV

"'Be careful for nothing,'" Benny said. "I'm careful about everything."

"Careful there means full of care. Worried." Odell tapped the card. "Paul ain't telling you to quit caring about that little girl. He's telling you where to take it."

"Who wrote this out?"

"Man named Hollis. Ran this shop before me. Sponsored me when I was about your age, back when I was the one nobody wanted at birthday parties." Odell almost smiled. "He gave me that card my first week. Keep going. Bottom part."

> "Finally, brethren, whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are lovely, whatsoever things are of good report; if there be any virtue, and if there be any praise, think on these things. Those things, which ye have both learned, and received, and heard, and seen in me, do: and the God of peace shall be with you."
> — Philippians 4:8-9 · KJV

Benny read the last line again. "'Seen in me.' Paul's bragging on himself?"

Odell pulled a pocket New Testament from his shirt, the cover held on with electrical tape. "Look what he says the chapter before."

> "Not as though I had already attained, either were already perfect: but I follow after, if that I may apprehend that for which also I am apprehended of Christ Jesus."
> — Philippians 3:12 · KJV

"He tells them flat out he ain't arrived," Odell said. "He was a prisoner when he wrote this letter. And when he first came to Philippi, they beat him and locked his feet in the stocks. Look here."

> "And at midnight Paul and Silas prayed, and sang praises unto God: and the prisoners heard them."
> — Acts 16:25 · KJV

"That's how the church in Philippi got its start. Those folks heard Paul teach and watched how he lived. That's what he means. Somewhere else he says it plain: 'Be ye followers of me, even as I also am of Christ.' Watch me follow Jesus. Then do it."

Benny chewed on that. "So if I do it right, God shows up."

"Other way around." Odell shook his head. "God showed up first. Paul's writing to folks the Lord already saved. Chapter one says, 'He which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ.' Jesus paid for you before you ever picked up a wrench. You don't earn Him by doing these things."

"Then why's it say 'do'?"

"'Cause knowing ain't the same as doing. Paul put the promise right next to the practice. So you do what you've seen, and you trust Him with the rest."

"What if I mess it up?"

"Then you'll be like me, and Hollis, and Paul." Odell picked up the brass jet and held it to the light. "Watch me long enough, you'll see me mess up. Then watch what I do next."

He set it down. "So. Tomorrow. Hollis did three things with a hard day. Pray about it now, with thanks in it. Think on what's true, not what you're scared of. Then do the next right thing you've seen done."

"What's true," Benny said slowly. "I'm sober today. Her mom said yes. And Kaylee asked her mom if I was coming."

"That's true. That's lovely, too. Write that down somewhere."

So Odell laid one greasy hand on the toolbox lid and prayed, short and plain. "Lord, You know this boy's scared. Thank You he's sober today. Thank You that little girl asked for her daddy. Keep his heart and mind in Christ Jesus tomorrow. Amen."

Benny's turn came. He didn't pray thanks very well, so he only said one. "Thank You she asked if I'm coming. Amen."

They put the carburetor back together. Benny turned the key. It cranked and cranked and wouldn't catch. His stomach dropped.

Odell reached under the seat and turned a little lever. "You shut the fuel off before you pulled the carb. Remember?"

It caught on the next try. Benny laughed for the first time all morning.

"Tomorrow," Odell said over the engine. "Bring the present. Be kind. Don't try to explain three years in one afternoon. And leave when the hour's up, even if it's going good."

"That what Hollis did?"

"That's what I did. Watched him first, though."

Sunday evening, Odell's phone buzzed.

Benny: "Went. Stayed the hour. She hugged me twice. Her uncle wouldn't look at me. Grandma sent cake home."

Odell: "Sounds about right. Proud of you. See you Saturday."

On the way home, Benny stopped at the dollar store for a pack of index cards. He wrote Philippians 4:6-9 in the truck, in letters not near as square as Hollis's, and taped it to the visor.

He put the rest of the blank cards in the glove box. Somebody would need one.

**Watch someone follow Jesus. Then do what you've seen.**

*You don't earn God's presence by doing. Christ already began the work. But Paul joins the practice and the promise: do what you have learned and seen, and the God of peace shall be with you.*

### Prayer

Father, thank You that You began a good work in me before I knew how to do anything right. Thank You for Jesus, who paid for me in full. I confess that I worry more than I pray, and I know more than I do. Today I bring You every care, with thanks. Turn my mind to what is true, honest, just, pure, and lovely. Give me faithful, trustworthy people to learn from, and help me do what I have learned and seen. When I fail, teach me to get back up and follow Christ again. Keep my heart and mind in Christ Jesus. God of peace, be with me today. In Jesus' name, amen.
