### Scripture

> "Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much."
>
> — James 5:16 (KJV)

### Story

The tomatoes behind the church on Cherry Street came in heavy that September, and Saturday mornings were for picking.

Dana liked the garden. Eight months sober, and this was one of the few places her hands always knew what to do. Dirt under her nails. Sun on her neck. A five-gallon bucket filling slow.

Ruth worked the next row over. Seventy-one, straw hat, humming a hymn a half step flat. She had been Dana's sponsor since week two, and she knew the difference between quiet and peaceful.

"You've picked that same plant three times," Ruth said.

Dana looked down. Bare vine. "Guess I'm thorough."

"Mm-hm." Ruth turned her bucket upside down and sat on it. Then she patted the one beside her.

Dana sat. A mockingbird on the fence ran through every song it knew.

"How you doing? Really."

"I'm good."

"That's what you said at group Tuesday. And the Tuesday before." Ruth pulled a small Bible from her apron pocket, the pages swollen from years of humid mornings. "Can I read you what I've been chewing on this week?"

Dana shrugged. Ruth took that as a yes.

> "Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much."
>
> — James 5:16 · KJV

"Confess your faults," Dana said. "Great."

"One to another. That's the whole church, honey, not just whoever's in trouble this week." Ruth closed the Bible on her finger. "I'll go first. You don't owe me one back. I just need it too."

Dana looked up.

"I haven't talked to my sister in thirteen months," Ruth said. "Everybody at church thinks I made my peace with it. I told them I did. Truth is, I keep a list in my head of everything she ever did to me, and I quit praying about her back in the spring because I didn't want God changing my mind." She let out a long breath. "There. That's mine."

"You?"

"Me. Seventy-one and still need Jesus every single morning."

The mockingbird went quiet. So did Dana, for a long time.

"Three weeks ago I bought a bottle," she finally said, fast, like ripping off tape. "I didn't open it. It's in my hall closet, behind my winter coats. I keep telling myself it's just in case."

"In case of what?"

Dana didn't have an answer. Her eyes stung. "I keep telling myself it doesn't count, since I didn't drink it."

Ruth didn't flinch. "How's it been, carrying that?"

"Heavy. I can't sleep. It's like it's watching me from the closet."

Ruth turned back to the Psalms and handed her the Bible. "David knew about that. Read it."

> "When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day long."
>
> — Psalm 32:3 · KJV

"Keep going," Ruth said. "Look where he took it."

Dana found the line.

> "I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid. I said, I will confess my transgressions unto the LORD; and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin."
>
> — Psalm 32:5 · KJV

"Unto the LORD," Dana said. "So telling you doesn't make me forgiven."

"No ma'am. Only God forgives sin, and He does it because Jesus already paid for it. Telling me doesn't buy you a thing. It just means you're not carrying it alone in the dark. And now I know how to pray for you."

Dana read James again. "'That ye may be healed.' I'm not sick."

"James wrote to believers among whom some were sick. Look right above it." Ruth pointed.

> "And the prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord shall raise him up; and if he have committed sins, they shall be forgiven him."
>
> — James 5:15 · KJV

"That's strong," Ruth said. "God really does raise people up. James just doesn't hand us a calendar. He wasn't writing about a bottle behind some coats, either. But he tells us what to do with what we've hidden. Confess it. Pray for each other. The healing is God's to give, in His time."

Dana read the last line out loud. "'The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.' Well, that lets me out."

Ruth laughed. "Read the next verse."

> "Elias was a man subject to like passions as we are, and he prayed earnestly that it might not rain: and it rained not on the earth by the space of three years and six months."
>
> — James 5:17 · KJV

"Elias is Elijah," Ruth said. "Same passions as us. Read his whole story in First Kings sometime. In another chapter he gets tired and scared like anybody. Righteous doesn't mean he never failed. It means he was right with God and walked with Him. And the only way any of us get right with God is Jesus."

She turned one more page.

> "Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them."
>
> — Hebrews 7:25 · KJV

"He's praying for me?"

"He ever lives to do it. So when we pray for each other, we're not praying alone."

So they prayed. Right there on two upturned buckets, dirty hands folded together. Ruth prayed for Dana's closet, her sleep, and her Tuesday group. Dana prayed for Ruth's sister, stumbling over the words, and Ruth cried harder at that than at anything else.

Afterward Ruth stood and brushed off her knees. "Now. Where'd you say those coats are?"

They drove to Dana's apartment with the tomatoes in the back seat. Dana pulled the bottle out from behind a puffy green coat she hadn't worn since February. Her hands shook a little at the sink. Ruth didn't take it from her. She just stood close.

It smelled sharp going down the drain. Then it was gone.

"Tuesday," Ruth said. "You tell the group."

"Tuesday." Dana rinsed the sink. "And you?"

Ruth looked at her phone for a long moment. "I'm starting to pray for my sister again. Tonight. And I'll ask the Lord about that phone call. You pray I don't chicken out."

Dana didn't feel brand new. She felt tired, a little shaky, and not alone.

That night her phone buzzed. Ruth: "Praying. See you Tuesday."

Dana typed back: "Praying for you too."

**Say it out loud. Then let somebody pray for you.**

*Hiding keeps you alone with it. Confession brings it to the Lord, who forgives through Jesus, and to His people, who can carry you in prayer.*

### Prayer

Father, thank You that You see what I have hidden and still call me to come. I confess the things I have kept in the dark. Thank You that the blood of Jesus cleanses me, and that He ever lives to intercede for His people. Give me courage to say it out loud to someone I trust. Teach me to pray for others, and to carry their burdens as they carry mine. Bring healing to my body and soul as You see fit, and help me trust You in the waiting. Keep me walking in the light today. In Jesus' name, amen.
