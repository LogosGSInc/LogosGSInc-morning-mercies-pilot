Tuesday, October 6, 2026

### Scripture

> "Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near: Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts: and let him return unto the LORD, and he will have mercy upon him; and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon."
>
> — Isaiah 55:6–7 (KJV)

### Story

The side door of the church on Elm Street was propped open with a cinder block, and the light coming out of it was the warmest thing in the parking lot.

Wes had been watching it for twenty minutes from the cab of his truck. Engine running. Heater on. Phone face down on the seat.

Fourteen months sober. Then on Thursday they buried his father, and on the drive home there was a gas station.

Four days later, here he was.

Close enough to smell the coffee.

Too ashamed to open his own truck door.

He had a plan. A few clean days. A shave. Some sleep. Come back next week looking like a man who had it together.

He turned his phone over.

Six texts from Carl, his sponsor.

He opened only the last one.

"Still here. Door's open."

Somebody knocked on the passenger window.

Carl stood there holding two foam cups, steam rising off both. Wes unlocked the door because he couldn't think of a reason not to.

Carl climbed in and handed him one.

"Saw your truck from the window. Figured you might be cold."

"I'm not coming in tonight."

"Didn't ask you to."

Carl blew on his coffee.

They sat a while.

The heater hummed.

"I'm gonna come back," Wes said. "Next week. When I've got a few days again. I'm not walking in there like this."

Carl nodded slowly. Then he pulled a small Bible from his jacket, the cover worn soft at the corners.

"Let me read you an invitation," he said. "Isaiah fifty-five."

He opened the Bible.

> "Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters, and he that hath no money; come ye, buy, and eat; yea, come, buy wine and milk without money and without price."
>
> — Isaiah 55:1 (KJV)

Wes almost smiled.

"Without money."

"Without payment," Carl said. "You don't purchase this invitation."

He turned the page.

"Now listen to this."

> "Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near: Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts: and let him return unto the LORD, and he will have mercy upon him; and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon."
>
> — Isaiah 55:6–7 (KJV)

"While He may be found," Wes said.

Carl nodded.

"It means don't put Him off. You don't have to make yourself presentable before you turn to Him. He's calling you now."

Wes stared at the light coming from the side door.

"I thought I needed a few good days first."

"That plan keeps you alone another week."

Carl tapped the open Bible.

"Look what the verse says. Forsake the way. Forsake the thoughts. Return unto the LORD. You don't clean yourself up and then come home. You turn and come home."

"I've turned around before."

"I know."

Carl paused.

"So have I."

Wes looked down at the page again.

"He will abundantly pardon."

"That's the promise."

Carl flipped back two chapters.

> "All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."
>
> — Isaiah 53:6 (KJV)

Wes read the line again.

"His own way."

"Same picture," Carl said. "Going our own way."

He pointed to the last part of the verse.

"And Isaiah says our iniquity was laid on Him. Jesus bore our sin. Your grief isn't a sin. Missing your father isn't a sin. But the things you did in the middle of that pain do not have to become the place where you stay."

Wes swallowed hard.

"I don't feel forgiven."

Carl looked at him.

"Then don't build your hope on the feeling. Build it on what God said."

He turned the Bible back to Isaiah 55.

"Return unto the LORD, and He will have mercy. That's where your assurance is."

For the first time that night, Wes bowed his head.

His voice came out barely above the heater.

"Lord, I don't know how to fix this."

He stopped.

Then tried again.

"I went my own way. I'm coming back. Have mercy on me."

Carl said nothing.

He didn't need to.

After a minute, Wes raised his head.

Carl asked quietly, "How much have you been drinking?"

"Whiskey. Four days. A lot."

"Then we're not guessing about what's safe."

Carl pulled out his phone.

"We're getting medical guidance tonight before you try to handle this on your own. I'll stay with you while we make the call. If they tell us you need to go in, we go."

Wes nodded.

They made the call from the truck.

Only after Wes had spoken with a medical professional and had a clear next step did Carl glance toward the church door.

"You still want to sit in for the last few minutes?"

Wes looked at the light again.

This time he opened the door.

He walked in late.

Nobody stopped to stare.

Somebody slid a folding chair out with his foot.

Near the end, the leader asked if anyone was coming back in.

Wes raised his hand.

It shook.

He kept it up.

A few people clapped.

Not loud.

Just enough.

He didn't feel brand new.

He felt tired, ashamed of some things, grateful for others, and more willing to receive help than he had been an hour earlier.

Next week could keep its plans.

Wes had already turned around.

**He is calling now. Turn around. Come home.**

*The invitation was never "come back when you have yourself together." It has always been "come."*

### What Isaiah 55:6–7 Means

Isaiah 55:6–7 is both urgent and merciful. The passage calls us to seek the LORD now, call upon Him, forsake sinful ways and thoughts, and return to Him. The promise is not that repentance purchases mercy. Mercy belongs to God, and He abundantly pardons the one who returns to Him.

Isaiah 53 gives the deeper foundation for that hope: our iniquity is laid upon the suffering servant. The New Testament reveals that saving work fulfilled in Jesus Christ. A sponsor can knock on a truck window. A church can open a door. A friend can sit beside you and help you seek wise care. None of them can save you. God alone saves.

The call, then, is not to make yourself worthy before coming. It is to stop delaying and return to the LORD who is calling.

### Prayer

Father, thank You that You call the thirsty and the broke, and that Your mercy is not something I can purchase or earn.

I have gone my own way. Today I turn back to You.

Thank You for laying my sin on Jesus and for raising Him from the dead. When shame tells me to hide until I look better, remind me that You are calling me now.

Give me courage to forsake what is pulling me away from You, to seek the help I need, and to walk through the doors You have opened.

Keep me near You today.

In Jesus' name, amen.

**Let us today: Pray, Repent, Abide.**
