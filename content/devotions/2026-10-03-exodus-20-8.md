## Exodus 20:8 Meaning: Why God Commands His People to Rest

### A Morning Mercies story on Exodus 20:8 (KJV)

Nate Ellison had not taken a day off in three years. He said it the way some men talk about a scar, half complaint and half medal.

His heating and air business had grown from one van to three. His phone buzzed through supper, through his son's ball games, through church on the rare Sundays he went. "If I stop," he told his wife, "it all stops."

So on a cold Sunday afternoon in January, he was kneeling in front of a dead furnace in Lorraine Whitfield's hallway.

Mrs. Whitfield was eighty-one, still in her church dress, her Bible open on the kitchen table. She brought him coffee he didn't have time to drink.

"Sunday call costs extra," he said.

"I figured," she said. "When's your day, Mr. Ellison?"

"My day?"

"Your day off."

He laughed. "Ma'am, I don't get one."

"I'm grateful you came. Getting my heat back is a kindness. But helping someone in trouble and never allowing yourself to stop are two different things."

She tapped the open page. "Listen to what God told His people: 'Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy.'"

"With respect, God didn't have to make payroll."

"No. He had to make the world. And look what He did after." She turned to the front of the book and read slowly. "'And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made.'"

Nate wiped his hands. "God doesn't get tired."

"That's my point. He didn't rest because He was worn out. He rested because the work was finished and good. He stopped first. Then He told us to."

She turned toward Moses. "When Moses repeated this commandment in Deuteronomy, he gave Israel another reason. They were to remember that they had been slaves in Egypt and that the LORD had brought them out. God commanded rest not only because He is Creator, but because He is Redeemer. He would not let the people He had freed build another life shaped like bondage."

"On the seventh day," she continued, "God taught His people to stop their ordinary labor. Children, servants, resident strangers, even the animals were included. Rest was mercy. It was worship. It was a reminder that they belonged to the LORD."

The furnace clicked. Nate didn't move.

"So let me ask you," she said gently. "Who owns you?"

He wanted a quick answer. He didn't have one. He thought of the missed games, the phone face-up at supper, the guilt he felt whenever he sat still. He had built something good, and somewhere along the way it had started giving the orders.

"I don't even know how to stop," he admitted.

"God didn't leave His people's rest to chance. He gave them a command." She slid the Bible toward him and pointed. "Now read what Jesus says."

He read it out loud, quieter than he meant to. "'Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.'"

"The Sabbath pointed beyond itself to Him," she said. "Only Jesus gives the rest your soul ultimately needs. But once you belong to Him, stopping isn't laziness. It's trust. It's you telling the world that God holds it up, not you."

He finished the furnace and charged her the regular rate. She tried to argue. He wouldn't let her.

Nate began making room for worship and rest. The following Sunday, he turned his phone off at the church door. His hands shook a little. He sat beside his wife through the whole service. That afternoon he lay on the living room floor while his son explained a video game he didn't understand, and he never checked the time.

He missed a call that day. A competitor took the job. He felt it. The business didn't fall apart, though some weeks the stopping was harder than others.

A month later, his newest technician, Luis, came in red-eyed. His baby had arrived two weeks early.

"Take Sunday," Nate told him. "Take Saturday too."

"Boss, you sure? We're behind."

Nate almost said, *If I stop, it all stops.* Instead he said, "We're not slaves here, Luis. Go home. Hold your boy."

Luis stared at him.

Nate smiled. "Someone reminded me that I belong to God, not to my work. I'll tell you about it sometime."

God rested first. We remember. Christ gives rest.

*May the Lord of the Sabbath give your weary soul His rest today.*

### Prayer

Father, thank You for resting first and for blessing the day You set apart. Thank You for bringing Your people out of bondage and for sending Jesus to give rest to the weary. I confess that I often live as if everything depends on me. Forgive my anxious striving. Teach me to stop, to worship, and to trust You with what I leave unfinished. Make my rest a quiet witness, and help me give rest to others. In Jesus’ name, amen.
