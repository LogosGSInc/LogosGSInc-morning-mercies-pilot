The dryer on the end had been broken for a year, so nobody used that bench but Marcus. At 2 a.m., the Suds & Go laundromat was the only place in town with the lights on and nothing to drink.

Forty days. He had reached forty days three times now. Tonight his phone had lit up with his ex-wife's name, and the old craving walked in like it still had a key.

“You folding or fighting?”

An old man in a faded work jacket was pairing socks at the next table.

Marcus almost lied. Instead he said, “Fighting. And losing. Every time I get close to forty days, it finds me. Same night. Same kind of text. Same lonely hour. It's like it knows me.”

The old man nodded. “It does. Name's Ellis. Paul wrote about that: ‘Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil.’ That word for wiles, *methodeia*, comes from the same Greek root as our word method. The enemy doesn't come at you by accident. He works a plan. He studies the gaps.”

“So I need to try harder.”

“No. You've been trying harder. That's the problem. What's your armor made of right now?”

Marcus thought. “Willpower. Promises. Shame, mostly. Every time I fall, I swear it's the last time.”

“Homemade armor,” Ellis said gently. “And he knows every gap in it. Paul didn't invent God's armor. He borrowed it from Isaiah.” He pulled a small, worn Bible from his jacket and read:

> “And he saw that there was no man, and wondered that there was no intercessor: therefore his arm brought salvation unto him; and his righteousness, it sustained him.”  
> — Isaiah 59:16 · KJV

“Wait,” Marcus said. “No intercessor. Nobody stepping in. That's me.”

“That's all of us. Keep listening.” Ellis read on:

> “For he put on righteousness as a breastplate, and an helmet of salvation upon his head; and he put on the garments of vengeance for clothing, and was clad with zeal as a cloke.”  
> — Isaiah 59:17 · KJV

He closed the Bible. “God looked at people just like us. No man strong enough. So He came Himself. He wore that armor first, and in Jesus He won the fight at the cross. When Paul says ‘the armour of God,’ he means God's own armor. You don't build it. You're clothed in it.”

Something in Marcus's chest cracked. “Then why do I keep falling?”

“Because you keep taking His armor off to fight in your own. And you keep fighting alone at two in the morning. That's not armor. That's exposure.” Ellis softened. “The verse right before says, ‘Be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might.’ His might, not yours. And Paul's word is stand. Not win it all tonight. Stand. The Holy Spirit puts the sword in your hand, which is the word of God, and He teaches you to pray when the plan starts coming.”

Marcus wiped his face. “I don't know how to start.”

“Tell God the truth tonight. Tell a person tomorrow.” Ellis wrote his number on a dryer sheet. “Paul wrote that letter in chains, under Roman guard. He studied armor every day. He knew chains weren't the end of his story. They aren't the end of yours.”

They prayed right there between the washers.

### Eleven months later

Marcus stopped for gas after his Thursday men's group. A young man sat on the curb by the ice machine, shaking, staring at a paper bag he hadn't opened.

Marcus sat down beside him. “Rough night?”

“I had six months,” the kid whispered. “Blew it Saturday. I'm garbage.”

Marcus knew that voice. Not the kid's. The other one. Scripture calls him the accuser of our brethren.

“I used to hear that same voice,” Marcus said. “It knew my schedule better than I did. Can I tell you how God's people beat it?” He opened his worn Bible to the back.

> “And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death.”  
> — Revelation 12:11 · KJV

“Not by being good?”

“Not by their record. By His blood. Jesus paid for Saturday too. Then they told the truth out loud. That's what I'm doing right now.” Marcus smiled. “I still have hard nights. I just don't face them without armor, and I don't face them alone.”

The kid looked at the bag a long time. Then he handed it over. Marcus poured it out on the asphalt, gave him his number, and drove him home.

Before the kid got out, Marcus passed on the words Ellis had given him, the ones he said every morning now:

**He wore it first. We stand in it. We tell it.**

*May the Lord clothe you in His own armor today, and may your standing become someone else's hope.*
