### Scripture

> "For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD, thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end."
>
> — Jeremiah 29:11 (KJV)

### Story

This story is fictional and illustrates the passage. The handwritten card in the story uses a familiar modern rendering of Jeremiah 29:11. All Scripture quoted as Scripture in this devotional is from the King James Version (KJV).

The index card on the bathroom mirror at the Willow Street house had started to curl at the corners.

Dana's sister had copied a familiar modern rendering from her Bible in purple ink the day Dana checked into the program: *For I know the plans I have for you. Plans to prosper you and not to harm you. Plans to give you hope and a future.*

Sixty-one days ago, Dana had read it and cried. This morning she read it and wanted to tear it down.

Her plan had been simple. Ninety days. Home by Christmas. Back with her two kids, who were staying with her mother across town.

Yesterday her counselor had said the words she'd been dreading. The treatment team recommended she stay longer. Through spring, at least.

She found Miss Lorraine, the house manager, out back on her knees by the raised bed along the fence, a paper sack beside her.

"Hand me that trowel, baby."

Dana handed it over harder than she meant to. "Did you know?"

"Know what?"

"That they want me here till spring."

Lorraine kept digging. "I heard."

"I told my kids I'd be home for Christmas. I promised them." Dana's voice cracked. "That card on the mirror says God's got plans to prosper me. It sure doesn't feel like it."

Lorraine sat back on her heels and held up something small and papery and white.

"Know what this is?"

"Garlic."

"Around here you plant it in the fall and you don't pull it till sometime next summer. Most of the work happens underground for a while." She pressed the clove point-up into the dirt. "Sit down a minute."

"I don't need a gardening lesson, Miss Lorraine."

"I know it. Sit down anyway."

Dana sat on the edge of the bed with her arms crossed. Lorraine wiped her hands on her jeans and pulled a small Bible from her back pocket, the cover held together with tape.

"That verse on your mirror is true," she said. "But you've been reading it like a greeting card. Let me read it to you like a letter."

"It's a verse."

"It's a verse inside a letter. Jeremiah sent it to people who had been taken from their homes and carried into Babylon. Their plans had been torn apart. Here's the first thing God told them."

> "Build ye houses, and dwell in them; and plant gardens, and eat the fruit of them;"
>
> — Jeremiah 29:5 · KJV

> "And seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away captives, and pray unto the LORD for it: for in the peace thereof shall ye have peace."
>
> — Jeremiah 29:7 · KJV

Dana laughed, but not kindly. "Plant gardens in Babylon. That's this place."

Lorraine didn't argue. "There were prophets going around telling those folks they'd be home real soon. God said not to listen to them. Then He told them the truth about the time."

> "For thus saith the LORD, That after seventy years be accomplished at Babylon I will visit you, and perform my good word toward you, in causing you to return to this place."
>
> — Jeremiah 29:10 · KJV

"Seventy years," Dana said quietly. "That's a whole life."

"It was hope for a whole people, across generations. Not a quick trip home for every person who heard that letter." Lorraine let that sit. "And right after He tells them seventy years, He says this."

> "For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD, thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end."
>
> — Jeremiah 29:11 · KJV

Dana leaned over to look at the page. "That's my verse."

"Same verse. Older words. Your sister's Bible says 'prosper.' Mine says 'peace.' The Hebrew word there is *shalom*—peace, welfare, well-being. It's bigger than money, and it isn't a timetable. And that 'expected end' carries the idea of a future and a hope."

"So God's not promising me Christmas."

"No, baby. He didn't promise them a quick way home either. He promised them something else."

> "Then shall ye call upon me, and ye shall go and pray unto me, and I will hearken unto you. And ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart. And I will be found of you, saith the LORD..."
>
> — Jeremiah 29:12-14a · KJV

Dana didn't say anything for a while. Then: "That letter was to Israel, though. Is it even for me?"

"The letter was theirs first. Their road home isn't a promise that your treatment ends on a certain day, or that everything turns out the way you want." Lorraine turned toward the back of her Bible. "But the God who spoke to them has not changed, and the New Testament tells us where our hope rests."

> "For all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen, unto the glory of God by us."
>
> — 2 Corinthians 1:20 · KJV

"In Jesus," Lorraine said. "He talked about a grain of wheat that has to fall into the ground and die before it bears fruit. He was talking about Himself. He died. They buried Him. And on the third day He rose. That's where your hope lives. Not on a calendar. In Him."

Dana picked up a clove and turned it over in her fingers.

"So what do I tell my kids?"

"The truth. Don't make them a promise God didn't make you. Make them the promises you can keep."

Lorraine handed her the sack. Dana planted the rest of the row.

### What This Verse Promises—and What It Doesn't

Jeremiah 29:11 was first God's word to Judah's exiles. Their promised return from Babylon does not pass to every reader as a guaranteed personal outcome. It does not promise that your hard season will end on your schedule, or that you will receive a certain job, relationship, treatment result, or earthly success.

It also does not turn a treatment plan into a message from heaven about your exact timeline. Wise counsel from the people caring for you can be a gift. Take it seriously.

What the passage does reveal is God's covenant faithfulness in a season that felt long and disorienting. His purpose toward Judah was not abandonment. He called the exiles to build, plant, pray, and seek Him while they waited.

Christians should not claim Judah's seventy-year promise as their own personal timetable. Our sure hope is deeper: God's promises find their Yes in Jesus Christ. Peter calls this a "lively hope," given "by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead" (1 Peter 1:3).

A long season is not proof that God has forgotten you. You can seek Him faithfully while you are still in it.

### Dana, That Evening

At seven o'clock, phone time, Dana called her mother's house.

Her daughter picked up first. "Are you coming home for Christmas?"

Dana closed her eyes. "Not by Christmas, baby. I need to stay a while longer and get well. But I'm going to call you every night I'm allowed. And I planted something out back today. I'll tell you when it comes up."

She hung up and cried in the hallway for a while. Nobody rushed her.

Later she stood at the bathroom mirror. She did not take her sister's purple card down. She just taped a second one under it, in her own handwriting:

*Thoughts of peace, and not of evil. Ye shall seek me, and find me.*

Not much showed above the dirt that week. Lorraine said to give it time.

**Plant the garden anyway. His thoughts toward His people are peace.**

### Today: Pray. Plant. Seek.

**Pray:** Pray for the place you are in, even if you did not choose it. (Jeremiah 29:7)

**Plant:** Do one small, faithful thing you will not see pay off this week. A meeting. A phone call. A page of Scripture. An honest conversation.

**Seek:** Read Jeremiah 29:4-14 aloud. Seek the Lord with your whole heart. Then tell a safe, trustworthy person—a sponsor, pastor, counselor, or doctor—the truth about where you are.

### Prayer

Father, I am impatient. I want out of this season sooner than it is coming. I have listened to voices that told me what I wanted to hear. Forgive me.

Thank You for Your faithfulness. Thank You that Your promises find their Yes in Jesus, who died and rose again.

Teach me to plant where I am. Help me pray for this place, take wise counsel from the people You have put around me, and seek You with all my heart today, right here.

In Jesus' name, amen.
