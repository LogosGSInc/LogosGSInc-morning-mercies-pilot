# Morning Mercy

## Proverbs 4:23

*"Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."* (Proverbs 4:23, King James Version)

The heart in Scripture is more than feeling. It is the command center of a person — where thought, will, and affection meet. Solomon does not tell his son to guard his wallet, his reputation, or his schedule first. He tells him to guard his heart, because everything else flows from that one source.

But before we can guard the heart, God must give us one worth guarding. He promised, *"A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you"* (Ezekiel 36:26, KJV). Guarding begins with receiving.

The Hebrew verb translated "keep" is *natsar* — a watchman's word meaning to guard, watch, and preserve. The phrase *mikkol-mishmar* means "above every other watch." Solomon is not saying try harder. He is saying stand a post here before any other post. And what flows from a well-guarded heart eventually shows up in plain sight. Jesus said, *"A good man out of the good treasure of his heart bringeth forth that which is good"* (Luke 6:45, KJV). What we protect within, we produce without.

**God gives it. We guard it. Life reveals it.**

### Prayer

Father, thank You for giving me a new heart in Christ. Teach me to guard it with diligence, not fear. Watch with me over my thoughts, my words, and my affections, so that what flows out of me today would honor You and bless the people You have placed near me. In Jesus' name, amen.

# Morning Mercies Book Devotional

## Proverbs 4:23 Meaning: Why the Heart Must Be Guarded

### A Morning Mercies story on Proverbs 4:23 (KJV)

Marcus set the broken hinge on the counter and rubbed the back of his neck. The gate to his backyard had come loose in the wind last night, and by breakfast, the argument with his wife had come loose too. He wasn't sure which had woken the children first.

"You look like a man carrying more than a hinge," Elias said, sliding onto the stool across from him. Elias came in every Tuesday for coffee filters and small conversation. Retired pastor. Retired everything, he'd say, except retired from listening.

Marcus shrugged. "The gate keeps swinging. I keep fixing it. It keeps swinging."

"Which gate?"

Marcus looked up. He hadn't meant it that way, but Elias always heard things twice.

"Both, I guess."

Elias nodded slowly and reached for the hinge. "There's an old proverb my father made me copy out when I was your age. He said Solomon told his son what he needed to hear first. *Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life.*"

"I've heard that," Marcus said. "On coffee mugs, mostly."

Elias smiled. "It's better than a mug. The Hebrew verb for *keep* is *natsar*. Guard it. Preserve it. And the phrase is stronger than 'try hard.' *Mishmar* is a watch, a custody, a post. Above every other guard, stand there. The word for *issues* means outgoings — the springs life flows from."

"So the heart is the spring."

"The heart is the source. Whatever is inside will find a way out. Through the gate."

Marcus turned the hinge in his hand. He thought about the tone he'd used at breakfast. About the sarcasm that had leaked into a joke the night before. About how he had told himself for months that he was fine, that the pressure at work was manageable, that no one saw the resentment growing quietly behind his ribs like a weed behind the shed.

"I don't know how to guard something I've already let get away from me," he said.

Elias set the hinge down gently.

"You don't have to make yourself a new heart, Marcus. That's the mercy of it. Solomon told his son to guard the heart. Centuries later, when Israel could not, God promised what wisdom alone could not produce: *A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.* That promise is fulfilled in Christ. You don't guard the heart to earn one. You guard the one He gave you."

Marcus was quiet.

"And the Lord Jesus made it plain," Elias went on. "*A good man out of the good treasure of his heart bringeth forth that which is good.* What comes through the gate tells the truth about what's in the yard."

Marcus thought about his children hearing him at breakfast. About what had been coming through the gate lately.

"So how do you guard it?"

"You start by admitting the gate's been open. You bring what's inside to the Lord before you bring it to the people you love. You watch what you feed it — what you scroll, what you rehearse, what you keep going back to at night. And you ask the Spirit to stand the watch with you, because you can't do it alone."

Marcus nodded. He picked up the hinge and looked at it a long moment.

"God gives it," Elias said quietly. "We guard it. Life reveals it."

Marcus walked out with a new hinge in a brown paper bag and a longer conversation with his wife already forming in his chest. He would apologize first — not because an apology would fix everything, but because the gate was his to tend, and he did not want another morning to pass with the wind carrying out what he had failed to guard within.

*May the Lord who gave you a new heart stand the watch with you today.*

### Prayer

Father, thank You for the new heart You gave me in Christ, not because I deserved it but because You are merciful. I confess that I have left the gate unwatched — in my words, my thoughts, and my affections. Teach me to stand the watch with You today. Guard what I feed my heart, and let what flows from it bring life to the people You have placed near me. In Jesus' name, amen.
