# Morning Mercy

## Psalm 40:1-3

"I waited patiently for the LORD; and he inclined unto me, and heard my cry. He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings. And he hath put a new song in my mouth, even praise unto our God: many shall see it, and fear, and shall trust in the LORD."

Anyone who has ever fallen back into an old sin, habit, fear, or despair knows the feeling David describes. The "horrible pit" is not decorative language. It is the place where your own footing will not hold, no matter how hard you try to brace yourself.

Notice what David does not say. He does not say he clawed his way out. He waited. He cried. God did the lifting. Lamentations tells us why we can cry at all: His compassions fail not; they are new every morning. Mercy is not the prize for climbing well. Mercy is what meets us while we are still in the mud.

And God does more than remove us from danger. He establishes our steps and puts a new song in our mouths. Rescue becomes witness. The comfort we receive becomes comfort we can carry to someone else.

**Out of the pit. Onto the rock. Into the song.**

### Prayer

Lord, You are the God who hears the cry from the pit. Thank You that Your mercies are new before I even ask. Lift me where I cannot lift myself, steady my steps, and put a true song in my mouth — one that points someone else toward the God who rescued me. In Jesus' name, amen.

# Morning Mercies Book Devotional

## Psalm 40:1-3 Meaning: Out of the Pit and Into a New Song

### A Morning Mercies story on Psalm 40:1-3 (KJV)

The all-night diner off Fifth Street was almost empty at 4:12 a.m. Caleb had been nursing the same cup of coffee for forty minutes, staring at the number saved in his phone as "Danny — DO NOT CALL."

Ninety-one days sober.

He had counted every one of them since waking in a hospital hallway with no memory of how he got there. Tonight, the old urge had come back hard enough to make ninety-one days feel like somebody else's accomplishment.

A man two booths away looked over the top of a worn Bible.

"Coffee that bad?" he asked.

Caleb glanced down at the untouched cup. "Coffee's fine."

The man nodded toward the phone. "Something else isn't."

Caleb almost told him to mind his own business. Instead he said, "You ever get tired of fighting the same thing?"

"More than once." The man closed the Bible but kept one finger inside it. "Name's Ray."

Caleb gave him his name and looked back at the phone.

Ray said, "I used to spend nights in places like this because going home meant being alone with whatever I was trying not to become again."

That got Caleb's attention.

Ray opened the Bible. "David knew the feeling. Listen to the whole thing."

He read slowly:

> "I waited patiently for the LORD; and he inclined unto me, and heard my cry.  
> He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings.  
> And he hath put a new song in my mouth, even praise unto our God: many shall see it, and fear, and shall trust in the LORD."  
> — Psalm 40:1-3 · KJV

Caleb gave a tired laugh. "Sounds nice. I don't feel like I'm on a rock."

"Maybe because you're reading it like David climbed there."

Caleb looked at him.

"He didn't." Ray tapped the page. "He waited. He cried. God heard. God brought him up. God set his feet. God established his steps. Even the song was something God put in his mouth."

Caleb's thumb moved away from Danny's number.

"So waiting means doing nothing?"

"No. Waiting means refusing to pretend you're your own rescuer."

Ray turned back a few pages. "And there is a reason you can wait without assuming God has forgotten you."

He read again:

> "It is of the LORD'S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not.  
> They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness."  
> — Lamentations 3:22-23 · KJV

Ray looked toward the dark windows. "Morning isn't here yet, but His mercy isn't waiting on sunrise."

Something in Caleb's chest loosened.

He picked up his phone, opened Danny's contact, and deleted it.

Ray didn't celebrate. He just took another sip of coffee.

Caleb stared at the empty space where the number had been. "What happens when tomorrow gets bad too?"

"Then you remember that mercy is new there too."

Ray turned to another passage.

> "Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort;  
> Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God."  
> — 2 Corinthians 1:3-4 · KJV

Caleb read the last line again.

"That we may be able to comfort them."

Ray nodded. "That's the part I missed for years. I thought rescue meant I finally got my life back. Sometimes God gives you more than that. He gives your pain somewhere holy to go."

"Meaning?"

"Somebody sat with me once when I was one decision away from going back. Years later, I found myself sitting with somebody else." Ray smiled. "Comfort received becomes comfort carried."

The waitress topped off Caleb's coffee. Outside, the sky had begun to thin from black to gray.

Caleb wrapped both hands around the mug.

"Out of the pit," Ray said.

"Onto the rock," Caleb answered.

Ray smiled.

Caleb looked toward the windows. "Into the song."

Nothing about the night had become easy. Ninety-one days were still ninety-one days, and morning would still ask him to walk into day ninety-two.

But for the first time in hours, Caleb was no longer measuring the strength of his own grip.

He was thinking about the hands that lifted.

*Mercy does not wait for morning to begin; it is morning, arriving new before you ever ask.*

### Prayer

Father, thank You that Your mercies are new before we even wake to need them. We confess how often we try to climb out of our pits by our own strength and how quickly our footing fails. Hear us when we cry. Lift us where we cannot lift ourselves, steady our steps in Your faithfulness, and give us a true song shaped by the comfort we have received, so that someone else may see Your mercy through us. In Jesus' name, amen.
