# Morning Mercy

## Psalm 40:1-3

"I waited patiently for the LORD; and he inclined unto me, and heard my cry. He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings. And he hath put a new song in my mouth, even praise unto our God: many shall see it, and fear, and shall trust in the LORD."

Every recovering addict, every relapsed believer, every parent white-knuckling their way through a 2 a.m. urge knows what David is describing here. The "horrible pit" is not decoration for a psalm — it is the real, sucking mud of a life that will not hold your weight no matter how hard you fight it.

Notice what David does not say. He does not say he clawed his way out. He waited. He cried. God did the lifting — not willpower, not shame, not a resolution. Lamentations tells us why God moves this way: His compassions "are new every morning" (Lamentations 3:22-23). Mercy was already available before David ever cried out.

The rock under David's feet was not just relief — it was a foundation, steady enough to carry a new song. And a rescued man rarely stays silent. He sings loud enough that others find their way out too, "that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble" (2 Corinthians 1:4).

Out of the pit. Onto the Rock. Into the song.

### Prayer

Lord, You are the God who hears the cry from the pit. Thank You that Your mercies are new before I even ask. Lift my feet onto Your rock, steady my steps, and put a true song in my mouth — one that points someone else toward the God who rescued me. In Jesus' name, amen.

# Morning Mercies Book Devotional

## Psalm 40:1-3 Meaning: Out of the Pit and Into a New Song

### A Morning Mercies story on Psalm 40:1-3 (KJV)

The laundromat on Fifth Street stayed open all night, which was the only reason Marcus was standing under its buzzing fluorescent lights at 4:12 a.m., feeding quarters into a dryer that didn't need his one shirt and pair of jeans. He wasn't there for laundry. He was there because his apartment felt like a mouth that wanted to swallow him, and the only thing standing between him and the number saved in his phone as "Danny — DO NOT CALL" was distance.

Ninety-one days sober. He'd started counting the day he woke up in a hospital hallway not remembering how he'd gotten there, and he'd been counting every day since, the way a man counts rungs on a ladder out of a well he fell into on purpose more than once.

An older man came in dragging a laundry bag, nodded once, and started loading a machine like he'd done it a thousand times. He caught Marcus watching the dryer spin nothing.

"Rough night?" he asked, not unkindly.

"Rough life," Marcus said, and regretted saying it to a stranger.

The man — Ray, he said his name was — sat down two chairs away instead of leaving Marcus alone, which Marcus noted but didn't have the energy to resent. "I used to sit in gas stations at this hour," Ray said. "Not for gas. Same reason you're here for laundry you don't have."

Marcus didn't answer, but he didn't get up either.

"Took me a long time to say it out loud," Ray went on, "but I was in a pit. Not a mood — a pit, with mud on the bottom that grabs your feet so you can't even climb the sides." He said it plainly, no shame and no pride in his voice. "There's a verse for that. David wrote it: 'He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings.'"

"You memorize Bible verses at laundromats?"

"I memorize the ones that saved my life." Ray smiled for the first time. "Notice David doesn't say he pulled himself out. He waited. He cried out. God did the lifting. Used to make me mad — waiting felt weak. Then I ran out of my own strength and found out waiting was the only honest thing left."

Marcus thought about the ninety-one days, how none of them had felt like strength so much as barely holding on. "So what, you just wait and it fixes itself?"

"No. You wait, and find the mercy was already there before you asked. There's another verse — 'It is of the LORD's mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning.' Whatever almost happened tonight — that mercy didn't run out overnight. It was renewed before your alarm went off."

Something in Marcus's chest loosened, not all at once, but enough that he noticed it. He didn't mention Danny's number, but he deleted it right there under the fluorescent lights while Ray pretended not to watch.

"Rock's not just relief," Ray said, folding a shirt. "It's a foundation. David didn't just stop sinking — God established his steps, steady enough to carry a new song. That's the part I didn't expect — not just rescue, but having something worth singing that somebody else needed to hear."

Marcus looked at him for a long moment. "Is that why you're telling me this at four in the morning?"

Ray shrugged, folding another shirt. "Paul said it plainer: God 'comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.' Somebody sat with me once. I'm just passing it along."

The dryer buzzed, empty and finished. Marcus didn't move to open it. He just sat there a while longer, in the mud-colored light of 4 a.m., feeling for the first time in ninety-one days like the ground under him might actually hold.

*Mercy does not wait for morning to begin; it is morning, arriving new before you ever ask.*

### Prayer

Father, thank You that Your mercies are new before we even wake to need them. We confess how long we try to climb out of the pit on our own strength, and how tired that makes us. Meet us in the mud tonight. Lift our feet onto Your rock, steady our steps, and give us a true song shaped by the comfort we've received, so we can offer it to someone else still sinking. In Jesus' name, amen.
