Saturday, October 10, 2026

### Scripture

> "The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly."
>
> — John 10:10 (KJV)

### Story

Miss Lorene kept fourteen sheep on six acres by the creek, and every one of them ignored Wade.

"Come on, girls," he called, rattling the feed bucket the way she did. "Come on."

Fourteen heads came up. Fourteen heads went back down to the grass.

Lorene laughed from the truck tailgate. "They don't know you yet."

"I been out here three Saturdays."

"They been out here their whole lives." She took the bucket and gave it one shake. "Come on, girls."

The whole flock came at a trot, bumping and bawling like she'd been gone a year.

Wade was nine months sober. His sponsor had told him to find something useful to do with his Saturdays, and a man at church said Miss Lorene needed fence work since her husband passed. So here he was, stretching new wire along the creek, where something had pushed through low under the bottom strand.

His phone buzzed on the tailgate. A number he'd never deleted.

Dusty: "river sat. got the cooler. like old times. you in?"

Wade read it twice and laid the phone face down.

Lorene didn't ask. She handed him a sandwich.

They ate a while before he said it. "Can I ask you something? Is this it?"

"Is what it?"

"Sober. Fixing fence. Church Sunday, meeting Tuesday, bed by ten." He wasn't trying to be ugly. "I used to have a life. Friends. The river. Now I count sheep that won't come when I call."

Lorene wiped her hands, reached through the truck window, and pulled out a Bible gone soft at the corners. She found the page and handed it over. "Read me that."

> "The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly."
> — John 10:10 · KJV

"I know this one," Wade said. "The thief's the devil."

"Folks mostly say so, and he fits the pattern. But back up. See who Jesus was talking to."

She turned back a page. Jesus had just healed a man born blind, and the religious leaders didn't like how it happened.

> "They answered and said unto him, Thou wast altogether born in sins, and dost thou teach us? And they cast him out."
> — John 9:34 · KJV

"They threw him out," Lorene said. "First time in his life that man can see, and the ones who were supposed to be shepherds throw him out of the fold for telling what happened. Now look what's next."

> "Jesus heard that they had cast him out; and when he had found him, he said unto him, Dost thou believe on the Son of God?"
> — John 9:35 · KJV

"Jesus went and found him. Then He turned to those leaders and started talking about sheep."

> "Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that entereth not by the door into the sheepfold, but climbeth up some other way, the same is a thief and a robber."
> — John 10:1 · KJV

Wade looked down the line of fence they'd been patching. "Some other way."

"A thief don't use the gate. He comes through a gap." She nodded toward the creek. "In that chapter, Jesus is contrasting Himself with false shepherds—folks who used the sheep instead of caring for them."

Wade almost grinned. "This fence is taking my whole Saturday."

"That fence is giving you something back." She tapped the page. "Plenty of good things cost you. Look what the thief does. Steal, kill, destroy. That's his mark. Whatever lies to you, uses you up, and leaves you worse. Takes your money, your mornings, your name. Then tells you it's your friend."

Wade turned the phone over. He didn't need to read it again.

"Now the other half," she said. "'I am come that they might have life.' What life?"

"Better than this, I guess." He nodded at the pasture, then felt bad for saying it.

She didn't take offense. "Keep reading. He tells you."

> "I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep."
> — John 10:11 · KJV

"And later that winter, at a feast in Jerusalem, He said it plainer."

> "My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me: And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand."
> — John 10:27-28 · KJV

"More abundantly ain't more stuff," Lorene said. "And it ain't a life with no trouble. Lord knows. At the heart of it, it's Him. Knowing Him, and Him knowing you. Jesus said it plain when He prayed: 'this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent.' That starts now. Not just when you die."

She closed the Bible on her finger. "The thief comes to take. The Shepherd came to give. Gave His own life first. Then gives life to the ones who follow Him."

"I don't feel like I got the more abundantly part."

"You said you used to have a life." She let that sit. "Did you?"

Wade thought about the river. The mornings he couldn't remember. Dusty, who never came by once those first thirty days.

"Not really," he said.

Lorene looked out at her sheep. "Look at them. They ain't got much. Grass. Water. A fence that holds. Somebody who knows their names."

"You named them?"

"Every one. That's Biscuit. That's Naomi." She pointed at the one chewing on the truck's mud flap. "That's the one I pray for."

Wade laughed for the first time all day.

"Jesus said His sheep 'shall go in and out, and find pasture,'" she said. "That's a full life for a sheep. Known, fed, and kept. Looks small from the road. It ain't small."

Wade picked up the phone. "What do I do with this?"

"What do you want to do with it?"

He took a long time. Then he typed, "Can't. Got plans." He blocked the number and put the phone in his pocket.

"I'll probably think about that river all week," he said.

"Probably. Then call somebody you trust when you do. That ain't weakness. That's a sheep staying close." She raised an eyebrow. "Got plans, huh? What plans?"

"Figured I'd ask you."

She smiled. "Saturday we're trimming hooves. Dirty work. My nephew Curtis comes. He's eleven years sober. Fried catfish after."

Wade said he'd be there.

They finished the fence by three. He pulled the last strand tight across the low place by the creek and twisted it off.

Saturday he came. Curtis showed him how to hold a ewe still without hurting her. After, Wade rattled the bucket. "Come on, girls."

Nothing. Old Biscuit looked at him a long moment, then went back to the grass.

"Give 'em time," Lorene said. "Took me a while to learn His voice too."

That night Wade read John 10 all the way through. Slow. Like learning a voice.

**The thief comes to take. The Shepherd came to give.**

*In John 10, Jesus contrasts false shepherds, who come to steal, kill, and destroy, with Himself, the Good Shepherd who gives His life for the sheep. The "more abundant" life He gives is not more stuff or an easy road. At its heart, it is eternal life: knowing Him, being known by Him, and following His voice. Jesus was not speaking about addiction; the recovery focus is an application.*

### Prayer

Lord Jesus, You are the Good Shepherd, and You gave Your life for the sheep. Thank You that You go looking for the ones others cast out. I confess that I have listened to voices that lied to me and used me up, and some days I still miss them. Show me the gaps in my fence, and give me courage to close them. Teach me Your voice, and help me follow You. Give me the life You came to give, the life of knowing You. Keep me close to Your people, and when the old voices call, help me reach for someone I trust. Thank You that no man can pluck Your sheep out of Your hand. In Jesus' name, amen.
